About this book

THE CONQUEST OF FEAR: Mastering Fear and Unlocking the Power of Courage and Fearlessness - Basil King - HQ Full Book.

"The Conquest of Fear" by Basil King is a spiritual and philosophical exploration of the nature of fear and its impact on human life. Written in the early 20th century, the book examines how fear dominates human thought and action, ultimately hindering one’s connection to God, life, and true self-expression. King, who was an Anglican priest, was deeply influenced by Christian teachings and New Thought philosophy, which emphasized the power of the mind and the spiritual potential within each individual. Through his book, King aims to provide readers with a framework to overcome fear by aligning themselves with the Life-Principle, which he identifies as God. The book is divided into eight chapters, each progressing through different aspects of fear, its relationship to life, and how one can transcend it through spiritual understanding and personal transformation. Below is a summary of the main themes in the book and a brief description of each chapter.

1. Fear and the Life-PrincipleIn this opening chapter, King introduces the reader to the core concept of the "Life-Principle," which he describes as the vital, divine energy that animates all living things. King argues that fear is not an inherent part of life but a learned response that clouds one's natural connection to this Life-Principle. By recognizing and tapping into this universal source of vitality, individuals can begin to overcome the fears that paralyze them and limit their potential.Key to this chapter is the idea that fear arises from a misunderstanding of life’s true nature. King emphasizes that life itself is inherently positive, abundant, and filled with potential, while fear is a distortion of this reality. Through a deeper understanding of the Life-Principle, King suggests that individuals can begin to reclaim their power and live without fear.

2. The Life-Principle and GodHere, King delves deeper into the connection between the Life-Principle and God, portraying God as the ultimate source of life and the creative force that governs the universe. King argues that fear is, in many ways, a product of human separation from God, or rather, the perception of separation. By understanding that God is not distant but is rather the essence of life itself, individuals can begin to dissolve the fear that comes from feeling isolated or abandoned in a chaotic world.King encourages readers to trust in God’s omnipresence and omnipotence, asserting that recognizing God's constant presence in one's life naturally dispels fear. The closer an individual aligns with God and the Life-Principle, the less room there is for fear to exist.

3. God and His Self-ExpressionIn this chapter, King introduces the idea that God's self-expression is manifest in all aspects of creation, including human beings. He suggests that just as an artist expresses their soul through their art, God expresses His essence through life, nature, and the human spirit. This divine self-expression is infinite and perfect, and fear arises when individuals fail to see themselves as part of this divine unfolding.King emphasizes that human life is an extension of God’s creative force and that by embracing this truth, one can move beyond the limiting power of fear. Understanding oneself as an instrument of divine expression allows individuals to align with a higher purpose and frees them from the existential anxiety that fear often brings.
