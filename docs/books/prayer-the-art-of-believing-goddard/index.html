GODDARD - PRAYER, THE ART OF BELIEVING: The Secret to Wealth and Success - Neville Goddard (1945).

Prayer: The Art of Believing by Neville Goddard is one of the most practical and psychologically rich works on the nature of prayer, belief, and conscious creation. Unlike traditional religious interpretations that view prayer as a request directed toward an external deity, Neville reframes prayer as an inner act of consciousness—a disciplined use of imagination and feeling to bring desired outcomes into reality. This book bridges spirituality and psychology, presenting a clear method for transforming life through mental and emotional control. Neville argues that belief is not passive; it is creative. What one accepts as true in consciousness must eventually externalize in experience. Prayer, therefore, is not about asking—it is about assuming and feeling the reality of what is desired. Each chapter builds a structured understanding of how thought, feeling, and belief interact to produce tangible results. What emerges is a system that empowers the individual to consciously direct their life, relationships, health, and success. Below is a detailed exploration of each chapter and its deeper meaning.

Chapter 1: Law of Reversibility

Neville begins with a powerful concept borrowed from physics and psychology: the law of reversibility. This law suggests that if a certain physical effect can produce a mental state, then that same mental state can produce the physical effect. For example, just as smiling can make one feel happy, feeling happy internally can produce a smile externally. Neville extends this idea to all aspects of life. If external success brings feelings of confidence and fulfillment, then consciously generating those feelings within can produce success outwardly. This principle becomes a cornerstone of his teaching. Instead of waiting for circumstances to change in order to feel better, one must reverse the process—feel the desired state first, and allow it to manifest. The implication is profound: the inner world is not a reflection of the outer world; rather, the outer world is a reflection of the inner state.

Chapter 2: Dual Nature of Consciousness

In this chapter, Neville explains that consciousness operates on two levels: the conscious and the subconscious. The conscious mind is the thinker, the chooser, and the director. The subconscious mind is the receiver and executor of impressions. The subconscious does not reason or discriminate—it simply accepts what it is given and expresses it in reality. Whatever is impressed upon it, whether positive or negative, becomes a condition in one’s life. Neville emphasizes that the relationship between these two aspects is critical. The conscious mind must deliberately choose thoughts and feelings that align with desired outcomes. When these are impressed upon the subconscious, they become reality. This chapter highlights the importance of mental discipline. Without awareness, the subconscious is filled with random or negative impressions, leading to unwanted experiences. With control, it becomes a powerful tool for creation.

Chapter 3: Imagination & Faith

Here, Neville brings together two essential elements: imagination and faith. Imagination is the creative faculty that forms mental images, while faith is the emotional conviction that those images are real. He explains that imagination alone is not enough. One must believe in the imaginal act—feel it as true and natural. Faith is what transforms imagination from a fantasy into a creative force. Neville teaches that faith is not blind belief, but loyalty to the unseen reality. It is the ability to persist in the assumption that what one imagines is already true, even if external evidence has not yet appeared. This chapter encourages the reader to trust their inner vision and remain faithful to it. Imagination sets the stage, and faith brings it to life.

Chapter 4: Energy & Power

In this chapter, Neville explores the nature of mental and emotional energy. He explains that attention is the channel through which energy flows. What one focuses on, one energizes. Thoughts and feelings are not passive—they are dynamic forces. When sustained and intensified, they gather power and move toward expression. This means that repeated focus on fear or doubt strengthens those conditions, while focus on success and fulfillment strengthens those outcomes. Neville emphasizes the importance of directing energy consciously. By controlling attention and sustaining desired feelings, one builds the power necessary for manifestation. This chapter reinforces the idea that creation is not random—it is driven by focused energy and emotional intensity.

Chapter 5: Law of Thought Transmission

Neville introduces a fascinating concept: thoughts are not confined to the individual mind—they can influence others. The law of thought transmission suggests that mental states can be projected and received across distance. He explains that when one vividly imagines another person in a certain state—happy, successful, cooperative—that image can influence their behavior. This is not manipulation, but alignment. By holding a clear and positive image, one contributes to the realization of that state. This principle extends the power of imagination beyond the self. It shows that relationships, interactions, and outcomes can be influenced through inner work. Neville encourages responsible use of this power, emphasizing that imagining good for others ultimately benefits oneself as well.

Chapter 6: Good Tidings

In this chapter, Neville focuses on the idea of “good news” as a state of consciousness. Good tidings are not merely events—they are internal assumptions of positivity, success, and fulfillment. He teaches that one must learn to accept good as natural and expected. Many people unconsciously expect negative outcomes, reinforcing them through belief. By reversing this tendency and assuming good, one begins to experience it. Neville also highlights the importance of inner conversations. The dialogue one maintains within shapes expectations and experiences. By cultivating thoughts of success, harmony, and abundance, one aligns with those realities. This chapter is a call to optimism—not superficial positivity, but a deep, sustained belief in favorable outcomes.

Chapter 7: The Greatest Prayer

The final chapter presents Neville’s ultimate teaching on prayer. The greatest prayer is not spoken—it is felt. It is the silent assumption that what one desires is already fulfilled. Neville describes this as entering a state akin to sleep, where the conscious mind is relaxed and the subconscious is most receptive. In this state, one imagines and feels the wish fulfilled with clarity and conviction. This act impresses the subconscious deeply, setting the creative process in motion. The key is to make the imaginal act so vivid and natural that it feels like a memory rather than a wish. The greatest prayer is therefore an act of identification. One does not ask for something—they become the person who already has it. This chapter beautifully concludes the book, offering a simple yet profound method for applying all the principles discussed.

Conclusion

Prayer: The Art of Believing is a timeless guide to the inner mechanics of creation. Neville Goddard presents a system that is both spiritual and practical, rooted in the understanding that consciousness is the only reality. The book’s message can be summarized in a few key principles:

Feel the end, and the means will follow

Impress the subconscious through belief

Direct attention and energy consciously

Remain faithful to the unseen reality

Assume the wish fulfilled

Neville removes the mystery from prayer and replaces it with method. Prayer is no longer a plea, but a practice—a deliberate act of imagination and feeling. For those willing to apply these teachings, the results can be transformative. Life becomes less about reacting to circumstances and more about creating them. The individual steps into a position of authority, shaping reality from within. Ultimately, Prayer: The Art of Believing teaches that belief is not something we have—it is something we do. And when done consciously, it becomes the key to freedom, success, and fulfillment.
