About this book

GODDARD - PRAYER, THE ART OF BELIEVING: The Secret to Wealth and Success - Neville Goddard (1945).

Prayer: The Art of Believing by Neville Goddard is one of the most practical and psychologically rich works on the nature of prayer, belief, and conscious creation. Unlike traditional religious interpretations that view prayer as a request directed toward an external deity, Neville reframes prayer as an inner act of consciousness—a disciplined use of imagination and feeling to bring desired outcomes into reality.This book bridges spirituality and psychology, presenting a clear method for transforming life through mental and emotional control. Neville argues that belief is not passive; it is creative. What one accepts as true in consciousness must eventually externalize in experience. Prayer, therefore, is not about asking—it is about assuming and feeling the reality of what is desired. Each chapter builds a structured understanding of how thought, feeling, and belief interact to produce tangible results. What emerges is a system that empowers the individual to consciously direct their life, relationships, health, and success.Below is a detailed exploration of each chapter and its deeper meaning.

Chapter 1: Law of ReversibilityNeville begins with a powerful concept borrowed from physics and psychology: the law of reversibility. This law suggests that if a certain physical effect can produce a mental state, then that same mental state can produce the physical effect.For example, just as smiling can make one feel happy, feeling happy internally can produce a smile externally. Neville extends this idea to all aspects of life. If external success brings feelings of confidence and fulfillment, then consciously generating those feelings within can produce success outwardly.This principle becomes a cornerstone of his teaching. Instead of waiting for circumstances to change in order to feel better, one must reverse the process—feel the desired state first, and allow it to manifest.The implication is profound: the inner world is not a reflection of the outer world; rather, the outer world is a reflection of the inner state.

Chapter 2: Dual Nature of ConsciousnessIn this chapter, Neville explains that consciousness operates on two levels: the conscious and the subconscious. The conscious mind is the thinker, the chooser, and the director. The subconscious mind is the receiver and executor of impressions.The subconscious does not reason or discriminate—it simply accepts what it is given and expresses it in reality. Whatever is impressed upon it, whether positive or negative, becomes a condition in one’s life.Neville emphasizes that the relationship between these two aspects is critical. The conscious mind must deliberately choose thoughts and feelings that align with desired outcomes. When these are impressed upon the subconscious, they become reality.This chapter highlights the importance of mental discipline. Without awareness, the subconscious is filled with random or negative impressions, leading to unwanted experiences. With control, it becomes a powerful tool for creation.

Chapter 3: Imagination & FaithHere, Neville brings together two essential elements: imagination and faith. Imagination is the creative faculty that forms mental images, while faith is the emotional conviction that those images are real.He explains that imagination alone is not enough. One must believe in the imaginal act—feel it as true and natural. Faith is what transforms imagination from a fantasy into a creative force.Neville teaches that faith is not blind belief, but loyalty to the unseen reality. It is the ability to persist in the assumption that what one imagines is already true, even if external evidence has not yet appeared.This chapter encourages the reader to trust their inner vision and remain faithful to it. Imagination sets the stage, and faith brings it to life.
