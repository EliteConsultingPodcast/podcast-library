Summary

Niccolò Machiavelli wrote The Prince around 1513; it was published in 1532. It is a short handbook on how rulers acquire and keep power, drawing on examples from ancient history and Renaissance Italy.

Machiavelli classifies types of principalities, discusses armies and the dangers of mercenaries, and analyses the qualities a ruler should display. He famously argues that it is safer to be feared than loved if one cannot be both, and that a prince must sometimes act against conventional morality to preserve the state.

The book is valued for its realistic analysis of power and human behaviour, and remains a key text on leadership, politics and strategy.

Key ideas

  • Power must be understood as it is, not as we wish it were
  • Rely on your own forces, not mercenaries
  • Reputation and perception matter
  • Adapt to fortune with boldness
