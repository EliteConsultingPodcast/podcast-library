Summary

Wallace D. Wattles' 1910 classic sets out to describe a 'science' of becoming wealthy, arguing that getting rich results from thinking and acting in a 'certain way' rather than from environment, talent or luck.

Wattles proposes that a formless substance underlies all things and responds to clear, persistent mental images held with faith and gratitude. He insists that this must be paired with efficient action in one's present work, giving more in use value than one takes in money.

A distinctive theme is his rejection of competition: Wattles urges readers to be creative rather than competitive and to leave everyone they deal with feeling better off. The book later influenced the modern 'law of attraction' movement.

Key ideas

  • Form a clear mental picture of what you want
  • Hold it with faith and gratitude
  • Act efficiently every day in your current position
  • Create rather than compete; give more value than you take
