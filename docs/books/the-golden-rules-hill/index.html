Summary

The Golden Rules gathers lesser-known essays and editorials by Napoleon Hill, many of them from the magazines he published around the time of the First World War. They show his ideas in an early, compact form.

The pieces address themes such as service, cooperation, self-confidence, the value of a definite aim and the ethic of treating others as you wish to be treated. Hill treats the Golden Rule not only as morality but as a practical principle of business and leadership.

Taken together the essays present success as inseparable from character: lasting achievement depends on the goodwill and trust one builds with others.

Key ideas

  • Service and fairness are practical business principles
  • A definite aim focuses energy
  • Self-confidence can be cultivated
  • Character underpins lasting success
