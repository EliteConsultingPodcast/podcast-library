Summary

Thomas Troward, a retired judge in British India, delivered these lectures in Edinburgh in 1904. They became a cornerstone of New Thought philosophy.

Troward distinguishes between objective and subjective mind, explains the creative role of thought and discusses the relationship between the individual mind and the universal mind. He uses a logical, almost legal style of argument.

His conclusion is that the individual can consciously cooperate with universal creative law through focused thought and expectation, an idea that influenced many later writers.

Key ideas

  • Distinguish objective and subjective mind
  • Thought is the creative starting point
  • The individual mind links to universal mind
  • Expectation shapes outcomes
