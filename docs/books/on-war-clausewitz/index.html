Summary

Carl von Clausewitz's On War, published in 1832 after his death, is one of the most influential works on military theory. The podcast covers its opening books.

Clausewitz defines war as a continuation of politics by other means, describes the 'friction' and uncertainty of real conflict, and introduces the trinity of passion, chance and reason. He also discusses the theory of war and the elements of strategy.

His focus on uncertainty, purpose and the link between ends and means has made the book relevant to business strategy and leadership as well as military thinking.

Key ideas

  • War serves political purposes
  • Friction and uncertainty shape every plan
  • Strategy links means to ends
  • Moral forces matter as much as material ones
