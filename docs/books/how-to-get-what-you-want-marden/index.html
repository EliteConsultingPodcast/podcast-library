Episode description

O.S.MARDEN - HOW TO GET WHAT YOU WANT: Master the Secret Formula for Success & Achievement - Orison Swett Marden (1917).

In this episode of the Secrets of Success podcast, we dive deep into a timeless masterpiece of personal development: How to Get What You Want by Orison Swett Marden, originally published in 1917. As the founder of "Success" magazine and a foundational figure in the self-help movement, Marden's work has inspired generations of thinkers, leaders, and dreamers. This high-quality full book narration brings his wisdom to life, offering practical strategies for manifesting your desires through mindset, faith, and action. Whether you're seeking career advancement, personal fulfillment, or spiritual growth, Marden's principles remain profoundly relevant in our fast-paced world. Join us as we explore the book's core message: success isn't about luck or external circumstances; it's about harnessing the inner power that resides in every individual. We'll break down each chapter, highlighting key insights, anecdotes, and actionable advice that can transform your life. By the end, you'll understand why Marden's ideas influenced icons like Napoleon Hill, Norman Vincent Peale, and Tony Robbins, and how you can apply them today.

Orison Swett Marden's journey from humble beginnings to becoming a beacon of motivation is a testament to his teachings. Born in 1848 in New Hampshire, Marden faced early hardships, including the loss of his parents and years of manual labor. Yet, through self-education and unyielding optimism, he rose to prominence as an author, editor, and hotelier. His establishment of Success magazine in 1897 popularized the idea that success is attainable for all through positive thinking and character development. How to Get What You Want encapsulates his philosophy, blending spiritual insights with practical guidance. Marden believed in a divine partnership—aligning human effort with universal laws—to achieve abundance. His influence extends far beyond his era; Napoleon Hill's Think and Grow Rich mirrors Marden's emphasis on definite purpose and visualization. Norman Vincent Peale's The Power of Positive Thinking echoes his call for optimism amid adversity. Earl Nightingale's motivational recordings, Zig Ziglar's sales philosophies, and even Tony Robbins' high-energy seminars all trace roots to Marden's foundational ideas. Dale Carnegie incorporated Marden's optimism into interpersonal skills, while James Allen's As a Man Thinketh aligns with his focus on thought as destiny. This book isn't just historical; it's a blueprint for modern success seekers navigating challenges like economic uncertainty or personal doubt. Now, let's delve into the chapters, where Marden unfolds his secrets step by step. Each one builds on the last, creating a comprehensive guide to self-mastery.

Chapter 1: Something Touched Him

The book opens with a captivating narrative about an inner awakening—a mysterious "touch" that stirs the soul from stagnation. Marden describes this as a divine spark, often manifesting as dissatisfaction with the status quo. He shares stories of ordinary people who, upon feeling this inner unrest, pivoted toward greatness. For instance, he recounts a young man who, disillusioned with his mundane job, heeded an intuitive call to pursue invention, leading to breakthroughs. This chapter urges listeners to pay attention to these promptings, viewing them as destiny's whispers. Marden warns against ignoring them, as complacency leads to regret. Instead, embrace the discomfort as a signal for growth. In about 150 words of profound insight, he sets the foundation: transformation begins internally, with a willingness to listen and act.

Chapter 2: How To Get What You Want

The title chapter is the heart of the book, outlining Marden's core formula: desire + belief + action = manifestation. He stresses clarity—vaguely wanting "success" isn't enough; define it vividly. Visualize outcomes as already achieved, he advises, drawing from real-life examples like entrepreneurs who mentally rehearsed their triumphs. Marden debunks luck, asserting the universe responds to focused intention. He encourages persistent effort, even in setbacks, with anecdotes of historical figures like Thomas Edison, whose failures fueled invention. This chapter, spanning practical steps like goal-setting rituals, empowers readers to take ownership. It's a rallying cry: what you want is within reach if backed by unwavering confidence.

Chapter 3: Playing The Glad Game

Inspired by Eleanor H. Porter's Pollyanna, Marden introduces "playing the glad game"—finding joy in every circumstance. This isn't naive positivity; it's a strategic mindset to combat negativity. He explains how gratitude shifts energy, attracting better opportunities. Through stories of people who turned tragedies into triumphs—like a bankrupt businessman who focused on remaining assets—Marden illustrates its power. He advises daily practices: list three "glads" in tough situations. This chapter combats modern cynicism, reminding us that optimism fuels resilience. In roughly 200 words, it transforms gratitude from a buzzword into a life-altering habit.

Chapter 4: Discouragement A Disease – How To Cure It

Marden diagnoses discouragement as a curable "mental disease" that erodes ambition. Symptoms include doubt and inertia; causes stem from negative influences or self-doubt. His remedies: surround yourself with uplifting people, affirm strengths, and act despite fear. He shares cures through examples, like a writer who overcame rejection by persisting. Marden emphasizes refusing self-pity, viewing setbacks as temporary. This chapter equips listeners with tools like positive self-talk, making it essential for anyone facing burnout. It's a motivational antidote, proving discouragement is conquerable with discipline.

Chapter 5: The Force That Moves Mountains

Here, faith emerges as the ultimate force. Marden references biblical miracles, like moving mountains, to show belief's creative power. He differentiates blind faith from informed conviction, using stories of inventors and leaders who visualized impossibilities into realities. Faith, he says, dissolves obstacles and attracts allies. This chapter inspires spiritual depth, urging alignment with one's higher self. In an era of skepticism, Marden's message resonates: faith isn't passive; it's dynamic energy for achievement.

Chapter 6: Faith And Drugs

Provocatively, Marden compares faith's healing to medicine's. He argues the mind's "expectancy" can cure ailments, critiquing overreliance on drugs. Through cases of placebo effects and mental recoveries, he highlights thought's pharmacy. This chapter promotes holistic health, blending spirituality with science. It's forward-thinking, anticipating modern psychosomatic research, and encourages harnessing inner resources for well-being.

Chapter 7: How To Find Oneself

Self-discovery is key, Marden asserts. He guides introspection: question societal roles, uncover talents. Stories of misaligned careers illustrate the cost of ignoring one's true self. Practices like journaling reveal purpose. This chapter fosters authenticity, vital for fulfillment. It's a call to live genuinely, beyond external validation.

Chapter 8: How To Attract Prosperity

Prosperity starts mentally, not materially. Marden teaches abundance mindset: expect wealth, eliminate scarcity thoughts. Anecdotes show generous givers attracting more. He defines prosperity broadly—health, relationships, joy. This chapter demystifies wealth, making it accessible through belief and preparation.

Chapter 9: Thinking All Over

Marden calls for a thought overhaul: replace defeatism with empowerment. He warns habitual negativity shapes destiny, advocating vigilant mental hygiene. Examples of transformed lives through mindset shifts inspire change. This chapter underscores: thoughts are blueprints for reality.

Chapter 10: Heart-To-Heart Talks With Yourself

Self-dialogue is mentorship from within. Marden advises honest, encouraging internal conversations to reaffirm goals. Stories of self-coached successes highlight its power. This chapter builds emotional intelligence, turning self-talk into a growth tool.

Chapter 11: Our Partnership With God

Closing profoundly, Marden views success as divine collaboration. Align desires with higher purpose through integrity and service. Faith and love amplify efforts. This chapter spiritualizes ambition, offering harmony and meaning. In conclusion, How to Get What You Want is a spiritual and practical roadmap to mastery. Marden's optimism, rooted in real-world application, continues influencing the self-improvement landscape. This episode isn't just a narration; it's an invitation to apply these secrets. Tune in to unlock your potential—because getting what you want starts with believing you can.
