Summary

The Law of Success (1928) is Napoleon Hill's original, comprehensive course on achievement, written before Think and Grow Rich and far longer. It is organised as sixteen lessons, each devoted to one principle that Hill identified in the careers of leading industrialists, inventors and statesmen.

The early lessons establish the foundation: the Master Mind alliance, a definite chief aim, self-confidence, the habit of saving and initiative with leadership. The middle lessons cover the creative and personal forces of imagination, enthusiasm, self-control, doing more than you are paid for and a pleasing personality.

The closing lessons deal with accurate thinking, concentration, cooperation, the lessons hidden in failure, tolerance and the Golden Rule. Together they form a full curriculum on how character, habits and clear thinking combine to produce lasting success. The podcast presents the course in three parts.

Key ideas

  • Success is a learnable system of sixteen principles
  • A definite chief aim gives every other principle direction
  • Doing more than you are paid for compounds over time
  • Failure is often a temporary defeat that carries a lesson
  • Cooperation and the Master Mind outperform individual effort
