AWAKENED IMAGINATION: How to Create Wealth, Success, and Your Ideal Life - Neville Goddard (1954).

The power which makes the achievement of aims – the attainment of desires – inevitable.

Awakened Imagination is one of Neville Goddard’s most profound and transformative works, offering a powerful interpretation of the creative force within every human being. At its core, this book teaches that imagination is not merely a mental faculty for daydreaming or fantasy—it is the very power of creation itself. Neville presents imagination as divine, the hidden identity of God within man, and the key to shaping reality consciously. This work is both philosophical and deeply practical. Through clear explanations, symbolic interpretations, and real-life applications, Neville reveals how individuals can move from passive observers of life to deliberate creators. Each chapter builds upon the last, guiding the reader to awaken to their true nature and apply the “law” of imagination to transform their world. Below is a detailed exploration of each chapter and its significance.

Chapter 1: Who Is Your Imagination?

Neville begins by confronting a fundamental question: What is imagination? Most people view imagination as something secondary—an escape from reality. Neville overturns this assumption entirely. He declares that imagination is the true identity of man, the divine presence within. Drawing from biblical symbolism, he equates imagination with God Himself, suggesting that the human imagination is not separate from the divine but is its very expression. This means that every act of imagining is an act of creation. The chapter challenges the reader to shift their self-concept. Instead of identifying with the body or external conditions, Neville urges the reader to identify as imagination itself—the unseen cause behind all visible effects. This realization is the foundation of all conscious creation. If imagination is God, then imagining becomes a sacred and powerful act.

Chapter 2: Sealed Instructions

In this chapter, Neville explores the idea that truth has always been available but hidden in symbolic form. Sacred texts, particularly the Bible, are described as “sealed instructions”—not meant to be taken literally, but interpreted psychologically. He explains that many stories in scripture are allegories of inner processes. When understood correctly, they reveal how imagination works and how reality is formed. The “sealing” refers to the fact that these truths remain inaccessible until one is ready to see beyond surface meanings. Neville emphasizes that awakening imagination requires insight and reinterpretation. The instructions are already within reach, but they must be “unsealed” through awareness. This chapter encourages readers to look beyond appearances and discover deeper meanings in both scripture and their own experiences.

Chapter 3: Highways Of The Inner World

Here, Neville introduces the idea that the inner world is vast, structured, and accessible. Just as there are roads and pathways in the physical world, there are “highways” within consciousness that connect different states of being. He explains that thoughts, feelings, and imaginal acts travel along these inner highways, eventually objectifying themselves as external reality. By understanding and consciously directing these inner movements, one can navigate life deliberately. This chapter highlights the importance of mental discipline. Random thinking leads to random results, while focused imagination leads to purposeful creation. Neville encourages the reader to become aware of their inner conversations and habitual thoughts, as these are constantly shaping their experiences. The “inner world” is presented not as abstract, but as the true realm of causation.

Chapter 4: The Pruning Shears Of Revision

One of the most practical and transformative teachings in the book, this chapter introduces the technique of “revision.” Neville explains that the past is not fixed—it exists within imagination and can therefore be changed. Using the metaphor of pruning shears, he suggests that undesirable experiences can be cut away and replaced with more desirable versions. By revisiting past events in imagination and altering them to reflect what should have happened, one changes their emotional and psychological impact. This revised version of the past then influences the present and future. Neville provides examples of how this technique can heal relationships, remove regret, and reshape life circumstances. The key idea is that imagination is not bound by time. By changing the inner memory, one changes the outer expression. Revision becomes a powerful tool for personal transformation and liberation from negative patterns.

Chapter 5: The Coin Of Heaven

In this chapter, Neville introduces the concept of the “coin of heaven,” which symbolizes the unity of thought and feeling. Just as a coin has two sides, successful manifestation requires both a clear mental image and a corresponding emotional state. It is not enough to think about what one desires; one must feel it as real. The emotional acceptance of a desire as already fulfilled is what gives it creative power. Thought provides the image, and feeling gives it life. Neville stresses that feeling is the secret. When one feels the reality of their desire, they impress it upon the subconscious mind, which then brings it into physical expression. This chapter teaches that imagination must be accompanied by conviction. Doubt weakens the creative act, while belief and emotional certainty strengthen it.

Chapter 6: It Is Within

Neville reinforces a central truth: everything one seeks is already within. There is no external power to appeal to, no outside force to manipulate. The source of all creation lies within the individual. This chapter challenges the tendency to look outward for solutions, validation, or fulfillment. Instead, Neville directs attention inward, where all possibilities exist in potential form. He explains that circumstances are merely reflections of inner states. By changing one’s inner assumptions, one changes the outer world. The realization that “it is within” empowers the individual to take full responsibility for their life. This teaching eliminates dependency on external conditions and places creative authority firmly in the hands of the individual.

Chapter 7: Creation Is Finished

One of Neville’s most profound metaphysical ideas is presented here: creation is already complete. All possible states and outcomes already exist in the infinite realm of imagination. Rather than creating something new, the individual selects and enters into a desired state. This selection is done through imagination—by assuming the feeling of the wish fulfilled. Neville compares this to tuning into a frequency. Just as a radio receives different stations, the mind can align with different states of reality. What one experiences depends on what they are tuned into. This concept removes the struggle from creation. There is nothing to force or build—only to accept and embody. By assuming the desired state, one naturally expresses it in the outer world.

Chapter 8: The Apple Of God’s Eye

In this chapter, Neville emphasizes the value and significance of the individual. To be the “apple of God’s eye” means to be deeply cherished and central to the divine. Since imagination is God, and man is imagination, each person is inherently divine and worthy. This realization transforms self-image and dissolves feelings of inadequacy or unworthiness. Neville teaches that recognizing one’s divine nature leads to confidence, clarity, and creative power. When one sees themselves as the center of their reality, they no longer feel victimized by circumstances.

This chapter encourages a shift from self-doubt to self-recognition. Understanding one’s true identity as imagination elevates both self-perception and life experience. The SearchThe final section addresses the human tendency to search endlessly for truth, happiness, or fulfillment outside oneself. Neville concludes that the search itself is misguided when directed outward. All that one seeks is already present within imagination. The journey is not about acquiring something new, but about awakening to what already is. He suggests that the search ends when one realizes their own creative power. Instead of seeking answers, one becomes the answer. Instead of looking for change, one becomes the cause of change. This closing message ties together the entire book: imagination is the source, the method, and the fulfillment. To awaken imagination is to awaken to one’s true nature and creative authority.

Conclusion

Awakened Imagination is not just a philosophical text—it is a guide to conscious living. Neville Goddard presents a radical yet empowering idea: that imagination is the only reality, and by mastering it, one can shape every aspect of life. Each chapter builds a cohesive system:

Recognize imagination as divine

Interpret hidden truths

Direct inner activity

Revise the past

Feel the wish fulfilled

Look within

Understand creation as complete

Accept your divine identity

The book ultimately invites the reader to awaken—to move from unconscious reaction to deliberate creation. It challenges deeply held beliefs about reality and replaces them with a vision of limitless potential. For those willing to apply its teachings, Awakened Imagination offers not just insight, but transformation.
