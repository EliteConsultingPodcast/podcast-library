Summary

The Meditations are the private notebooks of Marcus Aurelius, Roman emperor from 161 to 180 AD, written in Greek for his own guidance and never intended for publication.

Across twelve books he reminds himself of Stoic principles: to accept what is outside his control, to focus on his own judgements and actions, to act for the common good and to remember the brevity of life. The opening book is a list of gratitude to the people who shaped him.

The notes combine philosophy with practical self-discipline. Their honesty about anger, fatigue and duty has made them one of the most widely read guides to calm, responsible leadership.

Key ideas

  • Focus on what is within your control
  • Our judgements, not events, disturb us
  • Act for the common good
  • Remember mortality to keep perspective
