Summary

Charles F. Haanel's The Master Key System began in 1912 as a 24-week correspondence course and became one of the central texts of the New Thought movement.

Each week presents a lesson and an exercise on the relationship between the conscious mind, the subconscious and what Haanel calls the universal mind. Readers practise stillness, concentration and visualisation, gradually learning to hold a clear mental picture.

Haanel argues that thought is creative and that disciplined thinking, combined with action, shapes health, wealth and circumstances. The course format makes it more of a training programme than a book to be read once.

Key ideas

  • Thought is a creative force
  • Daily concentration exercises train the mind
  • Visualise clearly and hold the image
  • Harmony with universal principles brings results
