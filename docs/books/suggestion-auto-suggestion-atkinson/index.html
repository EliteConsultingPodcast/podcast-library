SUGGESTION & AUTO-SUGGESTION: The Hidden Mental Laws of Influence, Wealth & Personal Power – William Walker Atkinson.

What if some of the most powerful forces shaping your success, wealth, character, habits, and personal life operate silently beneath the surface of conscious thought?

In this fascinating episode of Secrets of Success & Wealth, we explore Suggestion and Auto-Suggestion by William Walker Atkinson, one of the most influential writers of the New Thought and early mental-science movements. Atkinson examines the hidden mechanisms through which ideas enter the mind, become accepted as beliefs, influence behavior, and eventually become part of a person's character and way of life. Suggestion is everywhere. It can come through words, repetition, authority, advertising, social environments, habits, education, expectations, and even our own internal dialogue. Atkinson argues that the mind is constantly receiving impressions—and that these impressions can influence the way we think, act, feel, and respond to the world. But the central idea of the work is even more powerful: suggestion does not only come from outside. We can also direct suggestion toward ourselves. This is the principle of auto-suggestion. By deliberately repeating ideas, mental images, expectations, and beliefs, a person can attempt to influence their subconscious patterns and develop new habits of thought. For Atkinson, this becomes a practical instrument for self-development, confidence, discipline, character, prosperity, and personal transformation. The book therefore moves from understanding how other people influence the mind to understanding how we can consciously influence our own.

CHAPTER 1. WHAT IS SUGGESTION

The journey begins with the fundamental concept of suggestion. Atkinson examines how ideas can enter the mind and influence thought and behavior, sometimes without conscious examination. Words, impressions, circumstances, and expectations can all become suggestions. Understanding this process is the first step toward becoming more conscious of the forces influencing your decisions and beliefs.

CHAPTER 2. SUGGESTION OF AUTHORITY

Authority has enormous psychological influence. People tend to give greater weight to statements coming from individuals they consider knowledgeable, successful, powerful, or trustworthy. Atkinson explores this principle and explains why perceived authority can strengthen the acceptance of an idea. The chapter provides important insight into leadership, persuasion, communication, and social influence.

CHAPTER 3. SUGGESTION OF HABIT AND REPETITION

Repetition gives suggestions increasing power. An idea encountered once may be forgotten, while an idea repeated again and again can become familiar and eventually influence behavior. Atkinson examines the relationship between suggestion, repetition, habit, and subconscious conditioning, showing why repeated thoughts can become deeply established mental patterns.

CHAPTER 4. SUGGESTION OF IMITATION

Human beings naturally observe and imitate others. This chapter explores how people unconsciously reproduce behaviors, attitudes, expressions, ambitions, and emotional patterns they encounter in their environment. Atkinson emphasizes the importance of the people and circumstances surrounding us because our environment can become a continual source of suggestion.

CHAPTER 5. INSTANCES OF SUGGESTION

Atkinson now demonstrates how suggestion appears in ordinary life. Social relationships, conversations, expectations, advertising, opinions, and everyday experiences can all influence the mind. Recognizing these examples makes the invisible process of suggestion easier to understand—and helps the listener become more aware of how ideas are formed and reinforced.

CHAPTER 6. SUGGESTION IN BUSINESS

Suggestion enters the world of commerce and wealth creation. Atkinson examines how confidence, presentation, reputation, communication, and perceived value influence business relationships. The chapter connects mental influence with sales, leadership, customer relationships, and commercial success, revealing the psychological dimension behind many forms of business persuasion.

CHAPTER 7. SUGGESTION AND CHARACTER

Character develops through repeated thoughts, experiences, habits, and beliefs. Atkinson explores how suggestions about one's own identity can gradually become part of one's personality. The chapter introduces the possibility of deliberately cultivating qualities such as confidence, courage, persistence, determination, and self-control through conscious mental training.

CHAPTER 8. SUGGESTION IN CHILDHOOD

Children are particularly receptive to the impressions surrounding them. Parents, teachers, family members, social environments, and repeated statements can leave lasting mental impressions. Atkinson examines the importance of early suggestion and how childhood beliefs can continue influencing attitudes and behavior later in life.

CHAPTER 9. SUGGESTION AND HEALTH

Atkinson explores the relationship between mental states, expectations, beliefs, and physical experience. He discusses how fear, confidence, expectation, and suggestion may affect a person's perception of health and well-being. His historical ideas reflect the mental-science thinking of his era and provide an important window into early theories concerning the relationship between mind and body.

CHAPTER 10. MASKED SUGGESTION

Some suggestions are direct and obvious; others are subtle. Tone of voice, implication, atmosphere, repetition, symbolism, and indirect communication can all influence perception. Atkinson calls attention to these less visible forms of suggestion and encourages readers to become more conscious of the ideas entering their minds.

CHAPTER 11. SUGGESTIVE THERAPEUTICS

The work now turns toward practical applications. Atkinson examines the use of suggestion as a method intended to influence mental and physical well-being. Positive expectations, constructive thoughts, and directed mental attention become tools through which individuals attempt to change their internal states.

CHAPTER 12. MENTAL CAUSE AND CURE

Atkinson goes deeper into his belief that mental conditions can contribute to physical and emotional experiences. Fear, expectation, belief, and persistent thought are examined as potential influences on well-being. The chapter presents the mind as an important area for investigation when attempting to understand patterns of distress and recovery.

CHAPTER 13. THERAPEUTIC SUGGESTION

The theory becomes increasingly practical. Atkinson discusses the intentional use of words, mental imagery, concentration, and expectation to direct thought toward desired states. Suggestion becomes a deliberate process rather than something that happens accidentally.

CHAPTER 14. SUGGESTIVE TREATMENTS

This chapter continues the practical exploration of suggestion. Atkinson considers methods involving affirmations, repetition, visualization, verbal instruction, and carefully structured mental environments. The central principle is that consistency and focused attention can strengthen the impression made upon the mind.

CHAPTER 15. “THE NEW METHOD”

Atkinson introduces a refined approach to suggestive influence. Instead of relying exclusively on direct commands or forceful repetition, this method emphasizes indirect and natural forms of influence. The chapter explores how suggestion can be integrated into ordinary thought and behavior so that new mental patterns become increasingly familiar.

CHAPTER 16. AUTO-SUGGESTION

The focus now turns inward. Auto-suggestion means directing suggestion toward oneself. Rather than simply receiving ideas from other people, the individual consciously chooses the thoughts and mental impressions they wish to reinforce. For Atkinson, this is one of the central keys to self-development.

CHAPTER 17. SELF-IMPRESSION

The ideas we repeatedly hold about ourselves can influence how we behave. Atkinson examines the importance of self-image and the impressions a person continually creates within their own mind. By changing these internal representations, the individual attempts to develop greater confidence, purpose, persistence, and personal power.

CHAPTER 18. AUTO-SUGGESTIVE METHODS

Atkinson presents practical methods for applying auto-suggestion. Repetition, affirmation, visualization, concentration, and emotional conviction become instruments for reinforcing chosen ideas. The objective is to make constructive thoughts increasingly familiar until they become more natural parts of the individual's mental life.

CHAPTER 19. CHARACTER BUILDING

Auto-suggestion is presented as a method for systematic character development. Qualities such as courage, discipline, persistence, optimism, determination, and self-confidence can be deliberately cultivated through repeated mental impressions. The chapter connects inner conditioning with the outward development of a stronger and more purposeful personality.

CHAPTER 20. HEALTH, HAPPINESS AND PROSPERITY

The final chapter brings together the central themes of the entire work. Atkinson connects suggestion and auto-suggestion with health, happiness, personal development, relationships, ambition, and prosperity. His larger message is that conscious direction of thought can become an important part of creating a purposeful life.
