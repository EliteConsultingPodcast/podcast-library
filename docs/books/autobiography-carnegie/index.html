Summary

The Autobiography of Andrew Carnegie, published in 1920 after his death, tells the story of a Scottish immigrant boy who became one of the richest men in the world.

Carnegie recounts his childhood in Dunfermline, his family's emigration to Pittsburgh, his rise from bobbin boy to telegraph operator and railway manager, and his move into iron and steel. He describes the people who helped him and the business principles he followed.

The later chapters cover his philanthropy, including libraries, education and peace initiatives, reflecting his belief that wealth should be used for the public good.

Key ideas

  • Work hard and seize every chance to learn
  • Choose good partners and trust capable people
  • Concentrate your efforts
  • Wealth carries a duty to give back
