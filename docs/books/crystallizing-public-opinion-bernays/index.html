Summary

Edward L. Bernays, often called the father of public relations, published Crystallizing Public Opinion in 1923. It was one of the first books to define the profession of the public relations counsel.

Bernays explains how public opinion is formed through groups, habits, leaders and media, and how an adviser can help organisations understand and influence it. He draws on psychology and sociology to explain why people accept or reject ideas.

The book is an early study of persuasion and communication, important for understanding modern marketing, politics and media.

Key ideas

  • Public opinion is shaped by groups and leaders
  • Understanding psychology is key to persuasion
  • Communication must be two-way
  • PR shapes how organisations are perceived
