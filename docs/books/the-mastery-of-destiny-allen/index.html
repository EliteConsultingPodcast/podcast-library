Summary

In The Mastery of Destiny (1909), James Allen extends the ideas of As a Man Thinketh into a fuller discussion of how deeds, character and destiny are linked. He argues that destiny is not fixed fate but the accumulated result of our own actions.

Allen discusses the training of the will, thoroughness in work, the building of character through repeated acts, the power of concentration and the importance of self-control. Each is presented as a step toward directing one's own life.

The book closes with an emphasis on meditation and inner discipline. The person who governs their mind, Allen writes, gradually becomes the master rather than the servant of circumstance.

Key ideas

  • Destiny is the sum of our deeds
  • Thoroughness in small tasks builds character
  • Will and concentration can be trained
  • Self-mastery precedes mastery of circumstances
