Summary

Émile Coué, a French pharmacist and psychologist, published this short book in 1920, describing the method of conscious autosuggestion he used with thousands of patients.

Coué argues that imagination is stronger than will: when the two conflict, imagination wins. He recommends repeating a general formula, famously 'Every day, in every respect, I am getting better and better', morning and evening, without effort.

The book includes accounts of patients and practical instructions. It remains an important early text on the power of suggestion and positive self-talk.

Key ideas

  • Imagination is stronger than willpower
  • Repeat simple positive formulas daily
  • Avoid forcing; let suggestion work gently
  • Self-talk influences health and mood
