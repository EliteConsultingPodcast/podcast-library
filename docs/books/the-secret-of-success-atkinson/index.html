THE SECRET OF SUCCESS: The Law of Attraction, Desire & Personal Magnetism — A Course of Nine Lessons on the Application of the Latent Powers of the Individual Toward Attainment of Success in Life — William Walker Atkinson (1908).

What if success is not simply a matter of luck, circumstances, or opportunity, but also of the powers within the individual that can be developed and directed toward a definite purpose?

Published in 1908, The Secret of Success by William Walker Atkinson is a nine-lesson course exploring the relationship between thought, desire, will-power, enthusiasm, individuality, personal magnetism, and achievement. Atkinson presents a philosophy of personal development based on the belief that individuals possess latent mental and personal capacities that can be cultivated to support their ambitions. An influential work in the history of New Thought, this book explores themes that later became central to modern success philosophy and Law of Attraction teachings. Atkinson examines how dominant thoughts influence behavior, how desire motivates action, how fear can undermine progress, and how confidence and personal magnetism affect relationships and opportunities.

LESSON I — THE SECRET OF SUCCESS

The opening lesson introduces Atkinson's fundamental principles of achievement. What distinguishes those who accomplish their objectives from those who remain passive? Atkinson examines the instinctive desire to succeed and the qualities associated with leadership, initiative, and purposeful action. He emphasizes the unfoldment of individuality: discovering personal abilities and expressing one's potential instead of merely following the crowd. Success begins with self-development. Recognizing one's strengths, developing confidence, and taking responsibility for personal progress establish the foundation for achievement.

LESSON II — THE INDIVIDUAL

The second lesson explores the nature of the individual and the distinction between individuality and personality. Atkinson asks what the Self really is and examines the relationship between our deeper identity and the characteristics through which we express ourselves. He introduces the concept of the Master in the Brain, the directing principle behind conscious thought and action. The lesson encourages readers to consider whether they consciously direct their mental activities or simply react to habits and external influences. For Atkinson, understanding oneself is essential to developing latent powers and exercising greater control over one's decisions and behavior.

LESSON III — SPIRITEDNESS

In the third lesson, Atkinson examines spiritedness as a quality associated with vitality, courage, determination, and self-confidence. Spiritedness involves recognizing one's capacity to act and direct one's life rather than surrendering to hesitation or passivity. Atkinson explores the assertion and recognition of mastery, emphasizing the importance of an inner attitude that supports purposeful action. This lesson highlights the difference between wishing for success and developing the qualities needed to pursue it. Confidence becomes meaningful when expressed through initiative, persistence, and action.

LESSON IV — LATENT POWERS

The fourth lesson focuses on the hidden capacities that Atkinson believes exist within every individual. He examines the roles of earnestness, enthusiasm, desire, and will-power in bringing these abilities into expression. Atkinson considers the nature of the Will and its influence on thought and behavior. Potential alone is insufficient; abilities must be awakened, disciplined, and directed toward definite objectives. The lesson encourages listeners to reflect on how concentration, determination, and sustained effort can transform unused capacities into practical achievement.

LESSON V — SOUL-FORCE

The fifth lesson explores soul-force through the energy of enthusiasm. Atkinson presents enthusiasm as more than temporary excitement. It can strengthen commitment, encourage initiative, and help transform intention into action. He also examines how enthusiasm communicates itself to others through behavior, speech, and personal presence. Genuine conviction and energy can inspire cooperation and encourage people to work toward shared objectives. This lesson connects inner development with outward influence, emphasizing the role of attitude in both personal achievement and human relationships.

LESSON VI — THE POWER OF DESIRE

Desire becomes the central subject of the sixth lesson. Atkinson examines it as a fundamental motive force that can either serve the individual or dominate their thoughts and behavior. His central distinction is that desire can be a useful servant but a poor master. Scattered wishes and constantly changing objectives divide attention, while clearly defined desires can provide direction and motivation. Atkinson explores how desire may be consciously cultivated and directed toward specific aims. Rather than allowing impulses to determine one's course, individuals can learn to concentrate their energies on meaningful objectives. The lesson provides an important principle of success: desire alone does not guarantee achievement, but a definite purpose can guide decisions, sustain effort, and encourage the actions necessary for progress.

LESSON VII — THE LAW OF ATTRACTION

The seventh lesson is particularly important for listeners interested in the Law of Attraction and its historical development. Atkinson presents attraction as a great law of Nature and explores the idea that dominant desires influence the direction of thought and experience. He introduces Desire Force, the mental influence that, within his philosophy, directs attention toward desired outcomes. He also examines Fearthought: the tendency to dwell on unwanted possibilities, anticipated difficulties, and feared outcomes. Atkinson argues that fear can influence the mind as powerfully as desire. Repeatedly concentrating on fears may weaken confidence, restrict choices, and discourage constructive action. Learning to recognize fear and redirect attention toward worthwhile objectives therefore becomes an essential part of mental discipline. For modern listeners, this lesson offers a historical perspective on ideas associated with attraction and manifestation. It also invites reflection on the practical ways thoughts influence decisions, behavior, and persistence, without requiring the assumption that thoughts alone control external events.

LESSON VIII — PERSONAL MAGNETISM

The eighth lesson examines personal magnetism and the qualities that make certain individuals appear especially influential or engaging. Atkinson explores the relationship between confidence, vitality, individuality, and the ability to attract attention or inspire cooperation. He suggests that personal influence involves more than physical appearance; it also emerges through behavior, communication, conviction, and self-confidence. This lesson connects personal development with leadership and social relationships. The qualities cultivated within an individual become visible in interactions with others and may influence the trust and cooperation they receive. Listeners interested in communication, professional relationships, and leadership will find valuable questions to consider about their own presence and interpersonal effectiveness.

LESSON IX — ATTRACTIVE PERSONALITY

The final lesson brings together the course's principal themes through an examination of the attractive personality. Atkinson explores how personality can be developed and identifies self-respect, confidence, and mastery as important foundations of personal influence. Rather than treating personality as permanently fixed, he presents it as something that can be cultivated through conscious effort. The lesson concludes the progression from self-understanding to outward influence: recognize the Self, develop latent powers, direct desire, strengthen will-power, cultivate enthusiasm, overcome fear, and improve the qualities expressed through personality. For Atkinson, personal magnetism represents the outward expression of inner development and contributes to the pursuit of success.

WHY THIS BOOK STILL MATTERS

More than a century after its publication, The Secret of Success offers a fascinating insight into the origins of modern personal development and Law of Attraction literature. William Walker Atkinson brings together ideas about individuality, mental discipline, desire, enthusiasm, fear, and personal influence. These themes continue to appear in contemporary discussions of success, leadership, confidence, and achievement. The book is particularly valuable for understanding New Thought, a movement that emphasized the relationship between mental life, personal agency, and human potential. Although some of Atkinson's explanations reflect the metaphysical assumptions of his era, his discussions raise enduring questions. How clearly have you defined your objectives? Are your thoughts supporting constructive action or reinforcing hesitation? Which abilities could you develop to improve your opportunities and relationships?
